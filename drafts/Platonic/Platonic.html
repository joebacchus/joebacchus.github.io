Icosahedron is the best platonic solid

In Japan House London, they show a whole bunch of things. This one time, I spotted behind the bottles of aesthetic soy sauce, what appeared to be a whole family of paper polyhedra. These were the work of Tomoko Azumi, and you could purchase a little kit to make some yourself. Instead of buying this, I rather shamefully went home and used ordinary printer paper to make them.

They turned out okay, flimsy replicas, but wouldn’t they be that much better if they were made of metal? UCL has a fantastic playground called the Institute of Making, where you can essentially learn whatever craftsmanship you want and build whatever you can imagine - they even have spare parts and scrap materials to use. Apart from carving a pencil and piece of wooden cheese for it to live in, this was the perfect opportunity for me to do some metalwork.

In the scrap pile, I was lucky enough to find a single sheet of copper, and so it was a simple matter of mapping the paper folding to metal. Obviously I had to pick the best Platonic solid to make, and that would have to be the Icosahedron, because it looks cool, and is the closest to a sphere. So in it’s honor, I birthed it as a football-sized monumement

You can’t glue metal, as far as I knew, so you have to use these mysterious exploding joints to connect the pieces.

Recently, I found the work of Max Brückner, which dates to the early 20th century.
